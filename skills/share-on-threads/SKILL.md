# Share on Threads

Threads is text-first, tied to an Instagram identity but culturally
its own thing — heavy with ex-Twitter journalists, writers, and tech
people, conversational and a little wry rather than corporate. Its
"For You" feed surfaces posts from accounts a reader doesn't follow
based on an interest graph and, especially, on replies and reposts a
post generates — a post that only collects likes stays mostly inside
the author's existing followers. Meta has also shown reduced organic
reach to posts carrying an outbound link, the same pattern as
Instagram and Facebook, which makes where the link sits a real
distribution decision, not a stylistic one.

## What works on Threads

- **Replies and reposts drive reach, not likes.** The For You algorithm
  leans on conversation signals; a post phrased to invite an actual
  reply travels further than one that only invites agreement.
- **The link penalty is real.** A post with a link in the body
  tends to get measurably less algorithmic reach than a link-free one
  — put the link in a reply to your own post instead, and say
  "link below" or similar in the main post.
- **Self-reply threading extends the 500-character limit.** Replying
  to your own post to add detail, a screenshot, or the link is a native
  pattern here, not a workaround — use it deliberately: post one is the
  hook and ask, the self-reply carries the link and any extra context.
- **Early replies extend a post's reach window.** Responding quickly to
  the first comments reads to the algorithm as an active, engaging
  post and tends to keep it circulating longer — the same pattern as
  Instagram, Threads' sibling app.
- **A conversational, slightly informal register fits.** This audience
  reads a lot of posts and is quick to scroll past anything that sounds
  like corporate copy; plain, first-person phrasing outperforms a
  polished announcement voice.
- **Hashtags do very little.** Threads has no meaningful
  hashtag-browsing culture comparable to Instagram — don't spend
  characters on them.

## Hashtags

Skip hashtags by default. Threads' discovery runs on the interest
graph and reply/repost signals, not tag search, so hashtags mostly sit
unread at the end of a post without adding reach — the same situation
as Bluesky, and unlike Instagram or Mastodon.

## Structure for a 500-character post

With 500 characters and a real link penalty, the main post should be
the hook and the ask; the link belongs in a reply, not competing for
space (or reach) in the post itself.

1. **First ~140 characters — the news itself, plainly stated.** What
   the project is and why it might matter to this reader — this is
   what shows in notifications and in a repost preview.
2. **One honest, personal line.** What prompted it or what you're
   still unsure about — the detail that reads as a person, not an
   announcement.
3. **One specific ask, phrased to invite a reply.** A pointed question
   ("does this hold up for your use case?") outperforms a generic
   "thoughts?" both for getting responses and for the algorithm's
   conversation signal.
4. **An honest, explicit invitation to repost** if it'd be useful to
   someone else — said plainly, not as a growth-hack line.
5. **The link, in a self-reply**, not the main post — noted in the post
   as "link below" or "🔗 in the reply."

## Key components

- **Humble framing over hype** — "I've been building this and I'm not
  sure I got X right" invites replies in a way "Excited to announce…"
  doesn't on a platform this allergic to corporate tone.
- **A reply-shaped ask** — a question specific enough to answer in one
  line, since replies (not likes) are what the algorithm rewards and
  what actually produces feedback.
- **The link moved out of the main post** — the single biggest
  structural difference from a platform like Bluesky, where a bare
  link in the post carries no penalty.
- **Responsiveness in the first hour** — treating early replies as part
  of the post's job, not an optional follow-up.

## Common pitfalls

- **A link in the main post body**, quietly capping the post's reach
  before anyone even reads it.
- **A vague ask** ("let me know what you think!") that produces likes
  but few replies, which is what the For You algorithm actually
  weighs.
- **Marketing-voiced phrasing**, which this specifically
  conversation-oriented, ex-Twitter-heavy audience is quick to scroll
  past.
- **Ignoring early replies**, missing the window where responding
  actually extends the post's algorithmic reach.
- **Spending characters on hashtags** that do little to nothing for
  discovery here.
