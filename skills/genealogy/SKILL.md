# Genealogy Hobby

Genealogy is the hobby of researching family history — tracing
ancestry, documenting relationships, and uncovering historical records
about one's family, aided today by extensive online records and DNA
testing.

## Starting the research

Genealogical research works best moving from the known to the
unknown: starting with yourself and working backward through
generations you can verify, rather than jumping straight to a distant,
uncertain ancestor. Interviewing living relatives early is valuable —
oral history and personal records (photos, letters) can preserve
information that's otherwise lost once older relatives pass away.

## Evaluating sources critically

Not all sources are equally reliable — a birth certificate is
generally more reliable than a family story passed down over
generations, and even official records sometimes contain errors.
Cross-referencing multiple independent sources (records, DNA evidence,
oral history) before treating a connection as confirmed avoids
building a family tree on a mistaken assumption.

## Organizing findings

Genealogical research generates a lot of material — documents, photos,
notes — that becomes difficult to manage without a system. Recording
sources for every fact (not just the fact itself) matters
significantly, since an unsourced claim is hard to verify or correct
later, and is a common regret among genealogists looking back at early
research.

## Common pitfalls

- **Not citing sources for findings** — an undocumented fact is much
  harder to verify or trust later, both for the researcher and anyone
  who inherits the research.
- **Assuming a same-name match is the correct ancestor** — common
  names produce false matches regularly; confirming a match with
  additional corroborating detail (dates, locations, relationships)
  avoids building on an incorrect connection.
- **Treating family stories as verified fact without corroboration** —
  oral history is valuable as a lead, but should be checked against
  records where possible rather than accepted uncritically.
- **Not backing up research** — years of research lost to a device
  failure or lost account access is an avoidable and common regret.
