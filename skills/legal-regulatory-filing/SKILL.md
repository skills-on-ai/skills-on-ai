# Legal Regulatory Filing

A regulatory filing is a required submission to a government regulator
— a disclosure, application, license renewal, or periodic report —
that an organization must make to stay compliant with applicable law in
a regulated industry or activity.

## Common categories

- **Periodic disclosures** — regularly required reports (financial,
  operational, or activity-specific) that keep a regulator informed on
  an ongoing basis.
- **Applications and licenses** — filings required to obtain or renew
  authorization to conduct a regulated activity.
- **Event-driven filings** — disclosures triggered by a specific event
  (a data breach, a material corporate change, an incident) rather than
  a routine schedule.

## Managing regulatory filing obligations

- **Maintain a filing calendar** — tracking every applicable deadline
  across all relevant regulators, since missing a deadline can trigger
  penalties independent of the underlying substantive compliance.
- **Assign clear ownership** — each filing needs an accountable owner
  who understands both the substantive requirement and the deadline.
- **Verify accuracy before submission** — regulatory filings are
  often treated as sworn or certified statements; inaccuracies can
  carry consequences well beyond a simple correction.
- **Retain filing records** — keeping proof of what was filed and when
  protects the organization if a dispute arises about compliance
  history.

## Common pitfalls

- **Missing a filing deadline** — many regulatory penalties attach to
  lateness itself, independent of the substance of the filing.
- **Filing inaccurate information under time pressure** — rushing a
  filing to meet a deadline, without adequate review, risks submitting
  incorrect information that then needs correction (and possible
  disclosure of the error itself).
- **Not tracking which regulators apply** — an organization operating
  across multiple jurisdictions or regulatory regimes can miss an
  applicable filing requirement it wasn't tracking.
- **Treating all filings as equally routine** — event-driven filings
  (like a data breach notification, see
  [[legal-data-privacy-compliance]]) often have much shorter deadlines
  and higher stakes than routine periodic filings.
