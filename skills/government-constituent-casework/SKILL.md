# Government Constituent Casework

Constituent casework is the work an elected official's office does to
help an individual constituent resolve a problem with a government
agency — a delayed benefit payment, a lost passport application, an
immigration case stuck in process. The office doesn't decide the case
itself; it uses the standing of the elected office to get a status, an
explanation, or a fair hearing from the agency actually responsible.
The specific role, authority, and title of the office doing this vary
by country and level of government — a US member of Congress's
district office, a UK MP's constituency office, and a local
councillor's casework function all do a version of this, each under
its own rules for what the office can and can't do.

## Key components

- **A clear intake** — exactly what the constituent needs, the agency
  and case or reference number involved, and any relevant deadline
  (a benefit appeal window, a visa expiration), captured up front
  rather than pieced together later from scattered emails or calls.
- **A signed privacy or authorization release** — the constituent's
  written permission for the office to contact the agency and discuss
  their case, obtained before any outreach to the agency happens.
- **A tracked status** — a record of what's been done, what's pending,
  and when the constituent was last updated, so the office can give
  the constituent a status even when there's nothing new to report.
- **A defined point of contact at the agency** — many agencies have a
  dedicated congressional or constituent-liaison office for exactly
  this kind of inquiry; routing through it is usually faster than a
  general inquiry line.

## Why the authorization release can't be skipped

Most government agencies are bound by privacy law that prevents them
from discussing an individual's case with anyone else, including an
elected official's office, without that individual's documented
consent. An office that calls an agency about a constituent's case
without a signed release on file is normally just refused any
information — the agency can't confirm the case exists, let alone
share its status. That refusal doesn't just delay things; it can waste
the entire cycle time constituent casework exists to save, since the
office then has to go back to the constituent, get the release signed,
and start the outreach over.

## Common pitfalls

- **No authorization obtained up front** — the office contacts the
  agency before getting a signed release, the agency won't discuss the
  case, and the whole effort stalls until the release is chased down
  and resubmitted.
- **Long silence mistaken for neglect** — a constituent goes weeks
  without an update even though nothing has actually gone wrong, just
  moved slowly on the agency's end; without a proactive status check-in,
  the constituent reasonably assumes the office dropped the case.
- **A relevant deadline missed at intake** — an appeal window or
  document expiration that wasn't captured up front turns a normal
  case into an unrecoverable one.
- **Case closed as "resolved" on a referral alone** — the office
  refers the constituent to the right agency contact and marks the
  case closed, without ever confirming the constituent's actual problem
  got fixed.
- **No agency reference number recorded** — without it, every follow-up
  call starts from scratch re-identifying the case to the agency.
