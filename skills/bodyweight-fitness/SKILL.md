# Bodyweight Fitness

Bodyweight training (calisthenics) builds strength, control, and
conditioning using only the body's own weight as resistance — push-
ups, pull-ups, squats, and their many progressions — requiring
minimal or no equipment, which makes it especially accessible.

## Progressions instead of added weight

Where weight training increases resistance by adding load, bodyweight
training progresses primarily by increasing the difficulty of the
movement itself — moving from a knee push-up to a standard push-up to
a decline or single-arm push-up, for example. Understanding a
movement's progression ladder lets a beginner start at an appropriate
difficulty and advance systematically, rather than attempting a
movement that's too advanced and building poor habits.

## Core movement patterns

Most bodyweight training builds around a small set of fundamental
patterns: pushing (push-ups and their variations), pulling (pull-ups,
rows), squatting, hinging (single-leg or bridge variations), and
core/anti-extension work (planks and progressions). A well-rounded
routine touches each pattern rather than over-focusing on one.

## Advantages and limits

Bodyweight training is highly accessible (minimal equipment, can be
done almost anywhere) and builds genuinely useful relative strength
and body control. Its main limitation is that some movements become
very difficult to progress further without external resistance (like
added weight) once a certain strength level is reached — many
practitioners eventually combine bodyweight work with some external
loading for continued progress.

## Common pitfalls

- **Skipping progressions and attempting advanced movements too soon**
  — this is a common cause of poor form and injury; working through
  appropriate progressions builds a safer, more durable skill base.
- **Neglecting pulling movements** — pull-ups and rows are harder to
  do without equipment (a bar) than pushing or squatting movements, so
  they're often under-trained relative to pushing work, producing
  muscular imbalance.
- **Treating bodyweight training as inherently "easier" than weights**
  — advanced bodyweight skills (single-arm work, levers) are extremely
  demanding; the perception of bodyweight training as a lesser or
  beginner-only discipline undersells its real ceiling.
- **Not tracking progress** — without external weight as an obvious
  metric, it's easy to lose track of whether progressions are actually
  advancing; tracking reps, hold times, or progression level keeps
  training purposeful.
