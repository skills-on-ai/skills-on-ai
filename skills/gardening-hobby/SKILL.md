# Gardening Hobby

Gardening is the practice of cultivating plants — vegetables, flowers,
herbs — for food, beauty, or simply the satisfaction of growing
something, and it scales from a single windowsill pot to a full
backyard plot.

## The fundamentals

Nearly every gardening problem traces back to one of four factors:

- **Sunlight** — most vegetables need 6+ hours of direct sun daily;
  many common failures (leggy, weak plants; no fruit) come from
  underestimating how much shade a spot actually gets through the day.
- **Soil** — healthy, well-draining soil with organic matter (compost)
  matters more than any single fertilizer product.
- **Water** — consistent, deep watering (encouraging roots to grow
  down) beats frequent shallow watering, which encourages shallow,
  drought-vulnerable roots.
- **Timing** — planting according to the local growing season and
  frost dates, not just when the mood strikes, determines whether a
  plant has enough time to mature.

## Starting small

A common beginner mistake is starting with a large, ambitious garden
and getting overwhelmed by the maintenance. Starting with a few pots
or a small raised bed, with a handful of forgiving plants (herbs,
tomatoes, lettuce), builds real experience before scaling up.

## Common pitfalls

- **Overwatering** — more houseplants and garden plants die from
  overwatering (root rot) than underwatering; checking actual soil
  moisture before watering beats watering on a fixed schedule.
- **Ignoring the local climate and frost dates** — planting too early
  in spring, before the last frost date, is a common cause of losing
  an entire planting overnight.
- **Underestimating sunlight requirements** — a spot that looks bright
  can still fall well short of the 6+ hours most vegetables need.
- **Neglecting soil health** — repeatedly planting in the same
  depleted soil without adding compost or rotating crops leads to
  declining yields over time.
