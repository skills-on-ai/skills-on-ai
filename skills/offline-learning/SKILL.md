# Offline Learning

Offline learning means studying where the internet cannot interrupt:
paper books, handwritten notes, or material downloaded in advance and
used on planes, trains, or in a quiet room. The benefit is the absence
of interruption, and the risk is arriving unprepared. This skill plans
what to download or bring, when to study, and how to make offline time
count.

## Input required

- **The subject** and the specific goal, such as a course, exam, skill,
  or topic of interest.
- **Where offline time occurs**: commute, flights, a library, a cabin, a
  desk with the phone away.
- **Current resources**: books, courses, podcasts, articles, language
  apps.
- **Devices**: e-reader, tablet, phone, laptop, and their storage and
  battery.
- **Time per session** and sessions per week.

## Questions to ask first

Ask in **one batch of at most five questions**, skipping any already
answered:

1. What do you want to learn, and by when or to what level?
2. Where and when will you be offline or device-free?
3. Which material do you already have, in paper or downloaded form?
4. Which devices could you use offline, and how much battery and storage
   do they have?
5. How long can you concentrate in one sitting?

## Method

1. **Match material to the setting.** For each offline slot, pick the
   resource that fits the length and the conditions:
   - Short commute or waiting: audio lessons, flashcards, a few pages.
   - Long flight or train: a chapter, a long article, or a lecture series.
   - Device-free desk: a paper book with a notebook.
2. **Prepare before leaving.** Download or bring everything in advance,
   then test it with the device in airplane mode. Check that the content
   truly plays or opens offline: some apps only keep it for a limited
   time or require periodic sign-in, so read the product's own offline
   rules. Charge devices and pack a charger or spare.
3. **Use paper where it helps.** Books and handwritten notes need no
   battery and have no notifications. Keep a pen and notebook with the
   book.
4. **Protect device-free time.** Pick a regular block, put the phone in
   another room or switch it off, and tell people when you will be
   unreachable. Do not rely on willpower alone; remove the phone from
   reach. For connected distractions on other days, see [[focus-blocker]].
5. **Make sessions active.** Reading or watching alone teaches less than
   working with the material. At the end of each session, write from
   memory the main points, then check against the source. Make questions
   for next time. Use [[deliberate-practice]] to practice the skill
   rather than only consume it.
6. **Queue questions for later.** When you hit something you want to look
   up, note it down and keep going. Batch the lookups for when you are
   back online.
7. **Capture and file.** After the session, move your notes into the
   system you trust; see [[reading-capture]].
8. **Review after a few weeks.** Did offline time happen? Did you finish
   the material? Adjust slot, length, or resource.

## Output format

- **Plan table:** slot, duration, material, format, prepared yes or no.
- **Pre-trip checklist:** downloads tested in airplane mode, battery,
  charger, paper backups.
- **Device-free block:** when, where, how the phone is removed.
- **Session routine:** study, recall, questions list.
- **Lookup list** for later.
- **Review date.**

## Ground rules

- **Do not assume offline availability.** Confirm what works offline in
  the user's own app or device settings.
- **Do not invent claims** that paper or offline study is better for
  everyone; describe it as a way to reduce interruption.
- **Safety first.** Do not study while driving, cycling, or crossing
  streets; use audio only where it is safe and legal.
- **Respect access.** Some users rely on screens for accessibility,
  such as screen readers or large print; do not push paper where it
  does not suit.
- **Respect licenses.** Download only what the service or owner
  permits.

## Self-check before answering

- Does every offline slot have material that fits it?
- Was offline use tested before leaving?
- Is the device-free block protected, with the phone out of reach?
- Does each session include recall, not just exposure?
- Did I link practice to [[deliberate-practice]]?

## Common pitfalls

- **Untested downloads** — the course will not play on the plane.
- **Overpacking** — too much material, none finished.
- **Passive consumption** — pages turn, nothing is retained.
- **Leaving the phone in reach** — the block collapses in minutes.
- **Dead battery** — plan charging or use paper.
- **No lookup list** — curiosity pulls the user back online.

## Example prompt

> I have a four-hour train ride each week and a long flight next month,
> and I want to learn basic statistics. Plan what to download or bring,
> how to use the time, and how to protect one device-free evening a week
> for study.
