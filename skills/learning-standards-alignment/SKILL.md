# Learning Standards Alignment

Learning standards alignment is the process of connecting instructional
content — a lesson, a unit, or a whole curriculum — to an externally
published set of learning standards, and documenting exactly where each
standard is addressed and how thoroughly. It answers "does this
curriculum actually teach what the standards require," not "does this
curriculum hang together well as its own sequence," which is what
[[curriculum-design]] covers.

## Key components

- **Standards inventory** — the specific external document being
  aligned to (state standards, Common Core, NGSS, a professional body's
  framework), pinned to its exact version, since standards get revised.
- **Alignment matrix** — a table mapping each standard to the specific
  lesson(s) or unit(s) where it's addressed, so coverage can be checked
  standard-by-standard rather than asserted in the abstract.
- **Depth of alignment** — not just whether a standard is touched, but
  at what cognitive demand (recall, application, analysis) relative to
  what the standard itself requires.
- **Gap list** — standards with no matching lesson, or matched only at
  a shallower depth than required, surfaced explicitly rather than left
  implicit in the matrix.
- **Audit trail** — documentation in a form a reviewer, accreditor, or
  administrator can actually check without re-deriving the mapping
  themselves.

## Alignment is not the same as coverage

A standard can appear in the alignment matrix next to a lesson and
still not really be met, if the lesson only touches the topic at a
lower cognitive demand than the standard specifies. A standard that
calls for students to "analyze and evaluate" a concept is not aligned
by a lesson that only has them recall a definition, even though the
same keyword appears in both. This is the most common way alignment
documentation drifts from what students actually experience: it's easy
to mark a standard as covered because a related term shows up
somewhere in a unit, without checking that the depth of instruction
matches the depth the standard demands.

The fix is to record depth alongside location in the alignment matrix —
a common scale is Webb's Depth of Knowledge (recall, skill/concept,
strategic thinking, extended thinking) — so a reviewer can see not
just that a standard was addressed, but whether it was addressed at
the level it requires. This also makes the gap list more useful: a
standard alignment can fail because no lesson touches it at all, or
because every lesson that touches it does so at too shallow a depth,
and those are different problems with different fixes.

## Common pitfalls

- **Checkbox alignment** — marking a standard covered because a related
  keyword appears somewhere in a unit, without verifying the actual
  cognitive demand matches what the standard requires.
- **Aligning after the fact** — building the curriculum first and
  mapping standards onto it afterward tends to produce plausible-looking
  matrices for standards that were never really designed for, rather
  than a curriculum built to actually meet them.
- **No depth tracking** — recording only whether a standard is present,
  not at what cognitive level, hides the gap between "touched" and
  "met."
- **Stale standards version** — continuing to align against a superseded
  version of the standards document after a revision, so the alignment
  documentation no longer matches what's actually required.
- **Uncoordinated alignment across teams** — different teachers or grade
  teams independently claiming the same standard is "owned" by their
  lesson, leaving true coverage unclear until someone cross-checks —
  see [[curriculum-mapping]] for catching this across a whole program.
