# Home Reset System

A reset is not a deep clean. It is a short, repeatable routine that
returns visible spaces to a baseline, so the home is always about ten
minutes from presentable. The system has two parts: a small daily reset
and a weekly rotation through zones. Deep cleaning is a separate,
rarer job.

## Input required

- **Home layout**: rooms, sizes, and shared spaces.
- **Who lives there** and who can do what, including children and
  housemates.
- **Time available** each day and each week.
- **Current state**: where mess builds up, what the user dislikes most.
- **Physical limits or conditions** that affect the work, such as pain,
  fatigue, or low energy. Adjust the routine rather than push through.

## Questions to ask first

Ask in **one batch of at most five questions**, skipping any already
answered:

1. Who lives in the home, and who will share this routine?
2. How many minutes a day and a week can you honestly give it?
3. Which spots become messy first, and which mess bothers you most?
4. What does "baseline" look like to you (be specific, room by room)?
5. Do you have a time of day that already works as a trigger, such as
   after dinner?

## Method

1. **Define baseline in writing.** For each main room, three to five
   visible conditions, for example "kitchen: sink empty, counters
   clear, floor free of crumbs". If it cannot be checked at a glance,
   rewrite it.
2. **Build the daily reset.** Choose a fixed trigger (after dinner,
   before bed) and a timer, with a default of ten to fifteen minutes.
   Typical steps: dishes and counters, return stray items to their
   places, wipe one surface, quick floor pass in the busiest spot,
   start or finish one load of laundry. Stop when the timer ends.
3. **Make zones for the week.** Divide the home into five to seven
   zones, such as bathroom, kitchen detail, bedrooms and bedding,
   floors, entry and drop zone, living areas, and one "flex" zone.
   Assign one zone to each weekday or two, with a ten-to-twenty-minute
   job list each.
4. **Write the checklists.** Daily list on one card. Zone lists
   short enough that no one needs to decide anything.
5. **Share the load.** Assign tasks by person, not by "whoever
   notices". Pair tasks with the least-liked job rotating if needed.
6. **Set a catch-up rule.** If a day is missed, do not double up.
   Resume with the next day's list, and let the weekly zone
   absorb the slack.
7. **Separate deep cleaning.** Put oven, windows, and similar jobs on
   a monthly or seasonal list, one per week at most.
8. **Review after two weeks.** Cut tasks that never get done, shorten
   anything that runs over, and change the trigger if it fails.

### Daily versus weekly versus deep

| Cadence | Purpose | Typical length |
|---|---|---|
| Daily reset | Return to baseline | 10 to 15 minutes as a default |
| Weekly zone | Maintain one area properly | 10 to 20 minutes |
| Deep clean | Periodic heavy jobs | Scheduled separately |

## Output format

- **Baseline description** per room.
- **Daily reset card**: trigger, timer, ordered steps.
- **Weekly zone schedule** as a table: day, zone, tasks, owner.
- **Deep clean list** with a suggested month for each job.
- **Two-week review prompt** with three questions.

## Ground rules

- **Short beats thorough.** If the routine takes longer than the
  user's stated time, cut tasks, not the time limit.
- **No shame language.** A missed day is a normal event; the system
  handles it.
- **Respect limits.** For pain, fatigue, or low mood that persists, make
  the routine smaller and suggest talking to a clinician if it is
  affecting daily life.
- **Cleaning product safety.** Do not suggest mixing cleaning
  products; follow the labels.
- **Do not impose a standard of tidiness.** Use the user's baseline.

## Self-check before answering

- Is the daily reset within the user's stated minutes?
- Is every task checkable and assigned?
- Is deep cleaning separated?
- Is there a catch-up rule for missed days?
- Does the plan fit the user's energy, not an ideal household?

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating reset as deep clean**: the routine grows until it is
  abandoned.
- **No trigger**: "sometime in the evening" becomes never.
- **Too many zones**: seven zones with long lists is a second job.
- **Everything has no place**: a reset cannot work if items have
  nowhere to return, which is the job of [[everything-has-a-home]].
- **One person doing it all**: resentment ends the system.
- **Perfectionism**: skipping when there is no time to do it fully.

## Example prompt

> Two of us in a small flat, working full time. Weeknights I have
> maybe fifteen minutes. Design a daily reset and a weekly zone
> rotation so the place stays presentable without a big Saturday clean.
