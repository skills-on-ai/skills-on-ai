# Volunteer Management

Volunteer management is the practice of recruiting, screening,
onboarding, scheduling, and retaining people who give their time
without pay. It shares some shape with paid-staff onboarding — people
still need training, expectations, and a point of contact — but the
underlying exchange is different: a volunteer can walk away at any
point with no financial cost to them, so the relationship has to earn
their continued time in a way a paycheck doesn't have to.

## Key components

- **Recruitment and role definition** — a specific, bounded role
  description (what the volunteer will actually do, how many hours,
  for how long) recruited against, rather than a vague general appeal
  for "help."
- **Screening** — a process matched to the role's risk level, from a
  short intake conversation for a one-off event to background checks
  and reference calls for a role involving vulnerable populations.
- **Onboarding and training** — enough orientation that a volunteer
  can do the role correctly and safely on their first shift, sized to
  the role rather than copied wholesale from paid-staff onboarding.
- **Scheduling and coverage** — a system for matching volunteer
  availability to actual need, with enough lead time that gaps surface
  before a shift goes uncovered rather than during it.
- **Recognition and retention** — a deliberate practice of
  acknowledging volunteer contributions and checking in on their
  experience, since an unpaid person who feels unseen has the lowest-
  friction reason of anyone in the organization to simply stop showing
  up.

## Why the exchange is different from paid work

A paid employee who has a bad week still has a financial reason to
show up the next one; a volunteer has none. That changes what actually
keeps a volunteer engaged: clarity about what's expected of them,
visible evidence that their time mattered, and a role that fits the
time they actually have to give, rather than the time the organization
wishes they had. An onboarding process built for paid staff — dense,
compliance-heavy, assuming a multi-year horizon — tends to overwhelm a
volunteer who signed up for four hours a month, and a scheduling system
that treats a no-show as a discipline problem rather than a signal that
the role or timing doesn't fit misses the actual lever available:
adjusting the ask, not enforcing it.

## Common pitfalls

- **Vague role, vague recruitment** — asking for "volunteers to help
  out" without a specific role and time commitment recruits people who
  don't know what they signed up for, and who leave as soon as the
  mismatch becomes clear.
- **Screening mismatched to risk** — either skipping screening for a
  role that involves vulnerable populations or money, or running a
  background check for a one-time event shift that never needed one.
- **Onboarding copied from paid staff** — a multi-hour orientation
  built for someone with a multi-year employment horizon overwhelms a
  volunteer who gave four hours.
- **No acknowledgment after the shift** — a volunteer who never hears
  whether their time mattered has no reason, beyond goodwill alone, to
  come back for the next one.
- **Treating a no-show as a discipline problem** — without pay on the
  line, a no-show is usually a signal the role or schedule doesn't fit,
  not a violation to enforce against.
