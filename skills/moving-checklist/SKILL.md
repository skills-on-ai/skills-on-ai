# Moving Checklist

A moving checklist is a phased plan for relocating to a new home: what
needs to happen weeks before the move, in the final week, on move day
itself, and in the weeks after. Its job is to make sure nothing that
only comes up once — canceling a utility, notifying a landlord,
redirecting mail — gets missed simply because it wasn't due until it was
already too late.

## Key phases

- **Weeks before** — notify the landlord of move-out (per the lease's
  notice period) or list the current home for sale; schedule utility
  disconnection at the old address and connection at the new one; book
  movers or a truck once the date is set; start decluttering so you're
  not paying to move things you're about to discard anyway.
- **Final week** — confirm logistics with movers or helpers (date, time,
  address, parking/elevator access); pack a separate essentials box (see
  below); finish any remaining packing with boxes labeled by room; settle
  final bills and confirm cancellation dates line up with the actual
  move-out date.
- **Move day** — walk the old place empty before handing back keys;
  keep the essentials box and important documents with you rather than
  loading them with everything else; do a final check of easy-to-forget
  spots (attic, garage, mailbox).
- **Weeks after** — update your address everywhere it's on file (see
  below); unpack priority rooms first (kitchen, bedroom, bathroom) rather
  than working through boxes in arrival order; confirm mail is actually
  being redirected and nothing is still going to the old address.

## Key components

- **Moving inventory** — a list of what's actually moving, split from
  what's being discarded, donated, or sold, decided during decluttering
  rather than on move day when there's no time to think it through.
- **Address-change list** — every account and service tied to your
  address: bank, employer, subscriptions, voter registration, insurance,
  driver's license/ID, mail forwarding, and anyone who mails you
  anything regularly. Built ahead of the move, not reconstructed
  afterward from memory.
- **First-night essentials box** — one clearly labeled box (or bag) with
  what you need before anything else is unpacked: toiletries, a change
  of clothes, chargers, basic tools, medications, and paperwork for the
  move itself. Packed last, unloaded first, never sealed in with the
  rest of the boxes.

## Common pitfalls

- **Address-change list forgotten until mail starts bouncing** — by the
  time a missed bill or a returned card surfaces the gap, real mail has
  already gone to the wrong address for weeks.
- **No separate essentials box** — the first night at the new place gets
  spent digging through sealed, unlabeled boxes looking for a toothbrush
  or a phone charger.
- **Timeline with no buffer** — built around a moving date that's
  treated as fixed, when moves routinely slip a few days for reasons
  outside your control (closing delays, mover scheduling, weather); a
  plan with no slack turns any slip into a scramble.
- **Utility cancellation and connection dates that don't line up** — the
  old address's service cancelled before move-out, or the new address's
  service not starting until after move-in, leaves a gap with no power
  or internet.
- **Decluttering left until move day** — deciding what to keep while
  movers are waiting means most of it gets kept, boxed, and paid for by
  the pound or the hour, only to be discarded later anyway.
- **Inventory never written down** — makes it hard to confirm nothing
  was lost or left behind, especially with a moving company involved.
