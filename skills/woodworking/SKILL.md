# Woodworking Hobby

Woodworking is the craft of building or shaping objects from wood —
furniture, boxes, cutting boards, and more — ranging from simple hand-
tool projects to power-tool-equipped home workshops.

## Getting started without a full shop

A meaningful first project doesn't require a garage full of power
tools. A basic set — a hand saw, chisels, a block plane, clamps, and
sandpaper — is enough to build real skill with joinery and finishing
before investing in power tools like a table saw or router.

## Wood selection and joinery basics

- **Softwoods vs. hardwoods** — softwoods (pine, cedar) are cheaper and
  easier to work but less durable; hardwoods (oak, maple, walnut) cost
  more and are harder to cut but produce more durable, refined results.
- **Grain direction** — cutting, planing, and sanding with the grain
  (not against it) produces cleaner results and reduces tear-out.
- **Joinery** — how pieces connect (screws and glue, dowels, dovetails,
  mortise-and-tenon) trades off strength, appearance, and skill
  required; starting with simpler joinery (butt joints with glue and
  screws) before attempting decorative joinery like dovetails is a
  reasonable progression.

## Shop safety is non-negotiable

Power tools (table saws, routers, planers) can cause serious injury.
Eye and hearing protection, push sticks/blocks to keep hands away from
blades, and taking time to understand a new tool before using it are
not optional extras — most serious shop injuries happen from skipping
a safety step to save a moment of time.

## Common pitfalls

- **Skipping safety equipment "just this once"** — this is how most
  serious power-tool injuries happen; consistency matters more than
  any individual precaution.
- **Buying too many tools before building skill** — a small, well-used
  tool set teaches more than a large, underused one.
- **Not accounting for wood movement** — wood expands and contracts
  with humidity; furniture built without accounting for this can crack
  or warp over time.
- **Rushing the finishing step** — sanding and finishing take real time
  and patience; rushing this last stage is a common way to undermine an
  otherwise well-built project.
