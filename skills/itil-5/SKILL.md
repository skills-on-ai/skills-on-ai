# ITIL 5

ITIL 5 is the version of the ITIL IT service management framework that follows ITIL 4. It was released by PeopleCert in 2026, in a phased rollout. Compared with ITIL 4, public descriptions emphasize two shifts: a stronger **product** orientation (lifecycle accountability for digital products, not only services) and **AI governance** (oversight of AI systems built into governance and risk structures). Sustainability is also described as a core concern.

## Input required

- The organization's current ITSM approach (ITIL 4, another framework, or none).
- Its mix of services, digital products, platforms, and AI systems.
- Where AI is used or planned, and who is accountable for it.
- The goal: adoption plan, gap analysis, training path, or governance design.

## Questions to ask first

1. Is the organization moving from ITIL 4, or starting fresh?
2. Which things are managed as products, and who owns each over its life?
3. Where is AI used in services or products, and who oversees risk and compliance?
4. Is certification (people or organization) a goal?
5. Which parts of ITIL 5 have been published and licensed for the organization to use?

## Method

1. **Confirm the source material.** ITIL 5 is released in phases. Work from the official PeopleCert and ITIL publications the user has, not from memory. Mark anything unconfirmed.
2. **Map the estate.** List services, products, platforms, and AI systems, with an owner for each.
3. **Apply the product emphasis.** For each product, define lifecycle accountability from idea to retirement, linking to [[product-management]] and [[product-roadmap]], and clarify how it relates to the services that deliver it.
4. **Apply AI governance.** Identify AI systems, assign accountable owners, define risk and oversight controls, and tie them into existing governance and [[risk-register]] practice. Check applicable law and organizational policy.
5. **Address sustainability.** Note environmental and social considerations in decisions where the framework calls for them.
6. **Plan the transition.** From ITIL 4, compare current practices to ITIL 5 guidance, decide what to keep, change, or add, and phase changes.
7. **Plan capability.** Choose training and certification paths from the official scheme as published.

## Output format

- Estate map: item, type (service, product, platform, AI system), owner.
- Gap analysis against the ITIL 5 material the user supplied.
- AI governance controls table: system, risk, control, owner, review date.
- Phased adoption roadmap with open questions.

## Ground rules

- Do not state ITIL 5 structure, practice names, or exam details beyond what the user supplied or official sources confirm. Public descriptions are mostly from training providers and may be promotional.
- Do not invent legal obligations; AI rules vary by jurisdiction.
- ITIL is guidance, not a certifiable organizational standard; for that see [[iso-iec-20000]].

## Self-check before answering

- Are product and AI governance points tied to named owners?
- Is every claim about ITIL 5 sourced or flagged unconfirmed?
- Does the plan respect the phased release?

## Common pitfalls

- **Relabeling services as products** without changing ownership or lifecycle accountability.
- **AI governance as a policy document only** with no controls or owners.
- **Assuming ITIL 4 practices carry over unchanged.**
- **Treating training certificates as organizational compliance.**

## Example prompt

"We run ITIL 4 for a SaaS company with two AI features. Draft a gap analysis and phased plan to adopt ITIL 5, emphasizing product ownership and AI governance."
