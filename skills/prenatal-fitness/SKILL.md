# Prenatal Fitness

Prenatal fitness is exercise adapted for pregnancy — generally safe
and beneficial for most pregnancies, with modifications that account
for the physical changes of pregnancy and individual medical guidance,
rather than treating pregnancy as a reason to stop exercising
altogether.

## Medical guidance comes first

Every pregnancy is different, and a healthcare provider's individual
guidance should take priority over general fitness advice, especially
given specific risk factors or pregnancy complications. Checking in
with a provider before starting or continuing an exercise routine, and
whenever something feels different or concerning, matters more here
than in general fitness contexts.

## Common modifications

- **Avoiding exercises flat on the back after the first trimester** —
  this position can, later in pregnancy, reduce blood flow; many
  programs modify or avoid it from a certain point onward.
- **Adjusting intensity for changing balance and joint laxity** —
  pregnancy hormones loosen joints and shift the body's center of
  gravity, which changes balance and increases the risk from movements
  that were previously routine.
- **Avoiding activities with high fall or contact risk** — activities
  with significant fall risk or potential for abdominal impact are
  generally modified or avoided during pregnancy.
- **Staying attentive to hydration and overheating** — pregnancy
  changes thermoregulation, making hydration and avoiding overheating
  during exercise more important than usual.

## The general benefit of staying active

For most low-risk pregnancies, staying appropriately active supports
better energy, mood, and physical preparation for labor and recovery
— exercise is broadly encouraged during pregnancy for most people, not
something to avoid by default, though the specific type and intensity
should be adapted as pregnancy progresses.

## Common pitfalls

- **Following general fitness advice without checking it against
  individual medical guidance** — pregnancy is highly individual, and
  a provider's specific guidance should take priority over generic
  advice.
- **Continuing a pre-pregnancy intensity level without adaptation** —
  as pregnancy progresses, intensity, balance-dependent movements, and
  positions often need adjustment.
- **Stopping exercise entirely out of excess caution** — for most
  low-risk pregnancies, appropriately modified exercise is beneficial,
  not something to avoid altogether.
- **Ignoring warning signs during exercise** — symptoms like dizziness,
  unusual pain, or reduced fetal movement warrant stopping and
  consulting a provider promptly, not pushing through.
