# Knitting Hobby

Knitting is the craft of creating fabric by looping yarn using needles
— a portable, relatively low-cost hobby that produces wearable and
decorative items, from a first simple scarf to complex garments.

## The two stitches that underlie everything

Nearly all knitting builds from two basic stitches:

- **Knit stitch** — the most basic stitch, producing a smooth "V"
  pattern on the front of the fabric.
- **Purl stitch** — the "reverse" of the knit stitch; combining knit
  and purl stitches in different patterns produces textures like
  ribbing (stretchy, common on cuffs) and stockinette (the smooth
  fabric most sweaters use).

Learning to knit and purl consistently, with even tension, is most of
what a beginner needs before tackling more complex patterns.

## Choosing a first project

A simple garter-stitch scarf (knit stitch only, every row) is a
common, forgiving first project — it builds muscle memory for holding
the needles and yarn and maintaining even tension, without the added
complexity of shaping or pattern-reading that a garment requires.

## Common pitfalls

- **Uneven tension** — pulling the yarn too tight or leaving it too
  loose produces uneven fabric; this evens out naturally with practice
  and isn't worth obsessing over early on.
- **Starting with a complex garment as a first project** — sweaters
  and other shaped garments involve reading patterns, counting stitches
  across many rows, and shaping techniques that are much easier to
  learn after a few simpler projects.
- **Not counting stitches regularly** — an accidentally added or
  dropped stitch, uncaught for many rows, can be frustrating to trace
  back and fix; checking the stitch count periodically catches this
  early.
- **Using yarn/needle combinations mismatched for the pattern** — using
  the wrong needle size for the yarn weight a pattern specifies changes
  the finished size and drape substantially.
