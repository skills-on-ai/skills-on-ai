# Legal Corporate Governance

Corporate governance, from a legal perspective, covers the structures,
rules, and duties that determine how a company is directed and
controlled — the legal relationships between shareholders, the board of
directors, and management.

## Key legal concepts

- **Fiduciary duties** — directors and officers generally owe duties of
  care (acting with reasonable diligence) and loyalty (acting in the
  company's interest, not a conflicting personal one) to the company
  and its shareholders.
- **Board authority and process** — the board's formal decision-making
  authority is typically exercised through documented resolutions and
  properly convened meetings, not informal consensus.
- **Shareholder rights** — rights to vote on major decisions, receive
  information, and in some structures pursue derivative claims on the
  company's behalf.
- **Corporate formalities** — maintaining the legal separation between
  the company and its owners (proper documentation, separate finances)
  matters for preserving limited liability protection.

## Why formalities matter

Corporate formalities aren't mere paperwork — courts can, in some
circumstances, disregard the legal separation between a company and
its owners ("piercing the corporate veil") when formalities are
consistently ignored, exposing owners to personal liability that the
corporate structure was meant to prevent.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating board resolutions as optional formality** — major
  decisions made informally, without proper board process and
  documentation, can be challenged later and undermine the legal
  protection corporate structure is meant to provide.
- **Directors acting on a conflict of interest without disclosure** —
  undisclosed conflicts can breach fiduciary duty even if the
  underlying decision was otherwise reasonable.
- **Neglecting corporate formalities** — commingling personal and
  company finances, or failing to maintain required records, risks
  exposing owners to personal liability.
- **Ignoring shareholder communication obligations** — failing to
  provide legally required information or voting opportunities to
  shareholders can create its own legal exposure.
