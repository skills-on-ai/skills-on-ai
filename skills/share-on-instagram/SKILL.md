# Share on Instagram

Instagram is visual-first — the image, carousel, or reel is what stops
the scroll, and the caption's job is to extend it, not replace it. The
feed truncates captions after roughly the first 125 characters behind
a "more" link, and the ranking algorithm weighs saves and shares more
heavily than likes, and comments more heavily than either — so a
caption that only invites a like is optimizing for the wrong signal.
Captions also aren't clickable, so any link has to live in the bio or
a link sticker, not in the text itself.

## What works on Instagram

- **The visual has to carry the announcement on its own.** A caption
  can't rescue a screenshot or slide that doesn't already make the
  project legible at a glance — design the cover image or first
  carousel slide as the actual headline.
- **Carousels and reels reach further than single static images.**
  Multi-slide posts and short video get more algorithmic distribution
  than a single photo; for an announcement, a short carousel (what it
  is, why it exists, how to try it) outperforms one dense slide.
- **Saves and shares outrank likes.** A caption that gives someone a
  reason to save the post for later, or send it to a specific person,
  is worth more to distribution than one optimized purely to be liked.
- **"Link in bio" is the expected pattern.** Say where the link is
  rather than pretending the caption can carry a clickable one; a
  Stories post with a link sticker is the one exception if it's
  available.
- **Alt text matters, and Instagram lets you set it explicitly** (not
  just via caption text) — use it for anyone using a screen reader,
  independent of the caption itself.
- **First-comment engagement boosts reach.** Replying to early
  comments quickly reads to the algorithm as an active, engaging post
  and tends to extend its initial reach window.

## Structure for a caption up to 2200 characters

The visual, not the caption, is what earns the stop; the caption's job
is to convert that stop into interest, a comment, or a save.

1. **First ~125 characters — the hook, self-contained**, since
   everything after it sits behind "more" for anyone scrolling past.
2. **A short personal line.** Why the project exists or what it took —
   the human detail a slide deck alone can't carry.
3. **What it is**, in plain terms, with line breaks between thoughts
   rather than one dense paragraph.
4. **One specific ask** — feedback on something concrete, or a
   question that's easy to answer in a comment.
5. **"Link in bio"** (or a note that a link sticker is in Stories), and
   an honest, low-key invitation to share if it's useful to someone.
6. **Hashtags**, placed at the very end or in the first comment.

## Hashtags

Hashtags are still Instagram's most direct discovery lever — they're
searchable and let a post surface to people who don't already follow
the account — though their reach impact has become secondary to
reels/carousel distribution in the main feed. Use three to eight tags
that are genuinely specific to the project's topic, mixing a couple of
smaller/niche tags (where a post can actually rank and get seen)
with one or two broader ones for reach; avoid maxing out near
Instagram's 30-tag cap, which reads as spam and adds little beyond
roughly ten. Place them as a block at the end of the caption or in the
first comment — both work, and a first comment keeps the caption
itself cleaner. Skip tags irrelevant to the actual content purely
because they're popular (e.g. #love, #instagood) — they draw
uninterested viewers and dilute engagement signal rather than helping.

## Key components

- **A cover slide or image that states the news on its own**, since
  many viewers will absorb that before reading a word of the caption.
- **Humble, personal framing** — a behind-the-scenes tone ("here's what
  I was unsure about") outperforms polished ad copy on a platform
  where overt self-promotion is easy to scroll past.
- **A concrete ask that's easy to answer in a comment**, since comments
  are the highest-value engagement signal here.
- **An honest share invitation**, phrased plainly rather than as a
  growth tactic.

## Common pitfalls

- **A caption written as if it carries the announcement alone**, when
  the visual is what most viewers actually respond to.
- **A hook that doesn't survive the 125-character truncation**, losing
  most scrollers before they ever tap "more."
- **Hashtag-stuffing** near the 30-tag limit with mostly irrelevant
  broad tags, which reads as spam and doesn't meaningfully help reach.
- **No alt text**, leaving screen-reader users with no way to know what
  the image shows.
- **A vague or missing ask**, collecting likes without the saves,
  shares, or comments the algorithm actually rewards.
