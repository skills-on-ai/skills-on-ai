# Travel Rewards

Travel rewards turn spending the user already plans into flights or
hotel stays. The sensible version is simple: one program, earned from
normal spending, paid off in full each month. The costly version is
chasing points through debt, fees, and unnecessary purchases. This
skill builds the first and warns away from the second.

## Input required

- **Country and home airport**, since card products, programs,
  consumer rules, and tax treatment of rewards differ. Check current
  official and provider sources.
- **Travel pattern**: how often, where, solo or family, flexible dates.
- **Everyday spending categories** and monthly size, roughly.
- **Credit situation**: whether cards are paid in full each month, and
  any debt carried. Never ask for numbers on cards.
- **Existing programs** where the user already has balances.

## Questions to ask first

Ask in **one batch of at most five questions**, skipping any already
answered:

1. Where and how often do you travel, and how flexible are your dates?
2. Do you pay every card off in full each month?
3. Which airlines, hotels, or alliances do you already use?
4. Roughly what do you spend monthly on groceries, transport, and bills?
5. Are you comfortable managing one or two cards, or want only one?

## Method

1. **Gate on debt.** If the user carries card debt or might, stop: any
   interest or fee will usually exceed what points are worth. Route to
   [[basic-budget]] first.
2. **Choose a goal.** A specific trip type (a yearly flight home, a
   family holiday) beats "collect points".
3. **Pick one program.** Choose either a flexible bank-points scheme or
   one airline or hotel group the user really flies or stays with.
   Concentrating spending usually beats spreading it, and fewer
   programs means fewer expiring balances.
4. **Compare the real cost.** Annual fees, foreign transaction fees, and
   minimum-spend requirements count against the value. Do not rely on
   remembered point values; look at the current terms and a sample
   redemption.
5. **Earn from normal spending only.** Put planned bills and groceries
   on the card if they are accepted without a surcharge. Never buy
   something to reach a bonus threshold.
6. **Set the payment rule.** Autopay the full statement balance from the
   bill account; see [[bill-autopay]].
7. **Redeem with a plan.** Check award availability and taxes or
   surcharges on the redemption, and compare against the cash price. Not
   every redemption is a good deal.
8. **Guard the balances.** Note expiry or inactivity rules, and set a
   calendar reminder.
9. **Review yearly.** Is the fee still earning its keep? If not, ask
   the issuer about a no-fee option before closing, and check effects on
   credit; see [[credit-check-system]].

## Output format

- **Verdict** on whether to start, with the debt gate result.
- **Chosen goal and program**, with the reasoning in three lines.
- **Earning plan**: which spending goes where.
- **Cost-benefit table**: fees against realistic redemption value, using
  figures the user supplies.
- **Yearly review date** and expiry reminders.

## Ground rules

- Never ask for card numbers, passwords, or account logins.
- Do not state point values, bonuses, fees, or program rules as fact;
  they change. Tell the user to check current terms.
- Never recommend carrying a balance, borrowing, or manufactured spend
  to earn points.
- Present products as examples only, never endorsements.

## Self-check before answering

- Did I check that the user pays in full before anything else?
- Is it one program with a stated goal?
- Is the annual cost compared to value the user would actually use?
- Is full autopay set, and are expiry dates noted?
- Did I avoid asserting country-specific rules?

## Common pitfalls

- **Paying interest** that erases the reward.
- **Chasing sign-up bonuses** with spending not otherwise planned.
- **Collecting many programs**, none reaching a useful balance.
- **Letting balances expire.**
- **Ignoring fees** on redemptions or foreign spending.
- **Treating rewards as income** and spending more because of them.

## Example prompt

> I travel home twice a year and always pay my card off in full. Set up
> a simple points strategy around one program, using only my normal
> spending, and tell me what fees to check before I apply.
