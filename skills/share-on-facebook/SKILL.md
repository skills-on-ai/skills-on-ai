# Share on Facebook

Facebook's ranking ("Meaningful Social Interactions") favors posts that
generate comments and shares between people who already know each
other over ones that only collect likes, and it has for years shown
reduced organic reach to posts carrying an outbound link versus native
text, photo, or video. The feed also truncates a post behind "See
more" after roughly the first few lines on mobile — the exact cutoff
varies by device and font size, so treat the opening line as the whole
hook, not a lead-in. The audience on a personal profile is friends,
family, and acquaintances rather than industry peers, which makes this
the most non-specialist audience of the platforms in this family —
plain language matters more here than technical precision.

## What works on Facebook

- **Groups are the real distribution mechanism.** A personal profile
  post mostly reaches people who already know the author; a relevant
  topic Group (the project's subject area, a professional community,
  an alumni or local group) is how the post reaches strangers who'd
  actually care — post there too, following each Group's
  self-promotion rules.
- **Comments and shares outrank likes.** The ranking algorithm weighs
  a comment or share between connected people far more heavily than a
  like, so a post that only invites a like is optimizing for the wrong
  signal.
- **Native photo or video outperforms a bare link.** A post with an
  attached image (or Reel) gets materially more reach and looks better
  in the feed than a plain link card; if there's any project
  screenshot or short demo clip, attach it directly.
- **Outbound links get less reach.** Facebook has consistently shown
  lower organic distribution to posts with a link in the body; putting
  the link in the first comment (and saying "link in comments" in the
  post) is the common workaround, though a personal-profile post with
  a bare link isn't penalized as visibly as a Page post is.
- **Tagging people or Pages who're actually relevant extends reach**
  into their networks too — a collaborator, a mentioned tool, or an
  organization involved — but only when it's genuinely relevant, not
  as a reach tactic.
- **A warmer, more casual register than LinkedIn is normal here**,
  including occasional emoji — this is the "friends and family"
  platform, and a post that sounds like a LinkedIn post reads as
  slightly out of place.

## Hashtags

Hashtags carry essentially no discovery weight on Facebook — there's
no meaningful hashtag-browsing culture the way Instagram has one, and
the algorithm doesn't use them as a ranking signal for the main feed.
Skip them by default; they add clutter without adding reach.

## Structure for a post (written short, even though the limit is huge)

Facebook's character limit is effectively unlimited, but nothing about
that helps reach — short, native, comment-inviting posts consistently
outperform long ones, and a long post still lives or dies on its first
line before "See more."

1. **First ~140 characters — the news itself, plainly stated**, in
   language a non-specialist friend or acquaintance can follow with no
   prior context.
2. **One honest, personal line.** Why it exists, what it took, or
   what's still uncertain — the detail that makes it a person posting,
   not an announcement.
3. **A photo, screenshot, or short clip attached natively**, since
   that's what the algorithm and the audience both respond to more
   than text alone.
4. **One specific ask.** A particular kind of feedback, an
   introduction to someone who'd find it useful, or a direct "if you
   know someone this would help, a share genuinely helps" — not a
   vague "thoughts welcome."
5. **The link** — bare if reach matters less than clarity, or noted as
   "link in comments" if maximizing organic reach matters more.
6. **Also post it to one or two genuinely relevant Groups**, adapted to
   each Group's own self-promotion norms.

## Key components

- **Plain, non-specialist language** — this is the broadest, least
  professionally-filtered audience of the platforms here, so jargon
  that would be fine on LinkedIn needs translating.
- **Humble framing over hype** — "I built this and I'm not sure I got
  X right" fits the friends-and-family register better than an
  achievement announcement does.
- **A concrete ask** — specific enough that a friend with five minutes
  can actually act on it, rather than just liking the post.
- **An explicit, low-friction share invitation** — said plainly, since
  this audience responds to directness rather than a growth-hack tone.
- **Group-native distribution**, treated as a separate step from the
  profile post, not an afterthought.

## Common pitfalls

- **A bare link with no native photo, video, or context** — this gets
  the least algorithmic reach of any post format on this platform.
- **Posting only to a personal profile** and skipping relevant Groups,
  which is where most of the actual stranger-reach comes from.
- **A LinkedIn-toned post** — achievement-announcement phrasing reads
  oddly formal to a friends-and-family audience.
- **Burying the news in a lead-up line** before the "See more" cutoff,
  losing most people who scroll past without expanding.
- **A vague ask** that produces likes but no comments, shares, or
  actual help.
