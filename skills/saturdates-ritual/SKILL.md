# Saturdates Ritual

A saturdate is a standing, protected block of time on a regular day,
usually Saturday, set aside for enjoying someone's company. "Saturdates"
is a coined name for the idea; this skill does not claim a source for
it or any proven benefits. The point is practical: a recurring slot
survives busy weeks better than good intentions. It suits a couple
first, and can be adapted to a child, a friend, or a solo date.

## Input required

- **Who it is for**: partner, child, friend, or self.
- **Time available**: day, start and end, how often.
- **Budget**: a monthly or per-date amount the user chooses.
- **Practical constraints**: childcare, work, health, mobility, travel.
- **Interests** of both people, and things they have never tried.

## Questions to ask first

Ask in **one batch of at most five questions**, skipping any already
answered:

1. Who is the date with, and how often could you realistically do it?
2. What day and time is protected, and what usually competes with it?
3. What is a comfortable budget per date?
4. Do you need childcare or other cover, and who provides it?
5. What did you both enjoy doing together before things got busy?

## Method

1. **Fix the slot.** Choose a recurring time and put it on both
   calendars as busy. Frequency can be weekly, every two weeks, or
   monthly; pick one that can be kept, not an ideal.
2. **Set the budget.** Agree an amount per date or per month,
   including childcare. Include free options so cost never cancels it.
3. **Arrange childcare.** Line up a regular sitter, a family swap with
   another household, or a time when children are elsewhere. Book it
   in advance for the next several dates where possible.
4. **Take turns planning.** One person plans each date and the other
   shows up without managing it. The planner picks within the agreed
   budget and ensures logistics such as bookings and transport.
5. **Phones away.** Agree a rule: phones off or in a bag, with
   an exception such as childcare contact. Decide it together rather
   than imposing it.
6. **Keep an ideas list.** Put suggestions in a shared note: walks,
   markets, classes, a game night, a new neighborhood, a picnic.
7. **Set the low-effort fallback.** For tired weeks, define a floor:
   takeout at home, a walk, or a film with phones away. A small date
   beats a cancelled one.
8. **Reschedule, don't skip.** If it must move, set the new time at
   once.
9. **Review occasionally.** Ask: do we both still look forward to this?
   Change the format if not.

### Adapting it

| For | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Child | Shorter, child chooses sometimes, one-on-one with no siblings |
| Friend | Alternate hosts, keep a recurring monthly slot |
| Oneself | A protected solo outing with no tasks or screens |

## Output format

- A one-paragraph **ritual statement**: who, when, how often.
- A **rules card**: budget, phones, planner rota, reschedule rule.
- A **fallback menu** of three low-effort dates.
- A **first six dates** list with planner assigned.
- A calendar entry text.

## Ground rules

- No claimed origin and no promised results; describe it as a habit
  the user chooses.
- Keep it fun. Serious relationship talk belongs in [[life-dinner]],
  not here.
- Both people agree to the rules; nobody is pressured into attending.
- Respect budget limits; never suggest debt for a date.
- If the relationship involves fear, control, or abuse, this is not the
  right tool; encourage contact with a qualified professional or a local
  support service.

## Self-check before answering

- Is the slot protected on calendars, with childcare sorted?
- Are the budget and fallback realistic for this user?
- Do planning duties alternate fairly?
- Did I avoid unsupported claims about benefits?

## Common pitfalls

- **Date night becomes errands or logistics talk.**
- **One planner always**, which turns fun into work.
- **Expensive defaults** that make cancelling easy.
- **Phones present**, interrupting the point of the time.
- **Cancelling when tired** with no fallback.
- **Using the date for conflict**; park that for a separate talk.

## Example prompt

> My partner and I have two kids and keep saying we'll do more
> together. Set up a Saturdates Ritual: every other Saturday, a
> modest budget, a sitter plan, phones away, and a rota so we take
> turns planning. Give me six dates and a low-effort fallback.
