# Competitor Analysis

A competitor analysis is a structured comparison of a company or
product against named, specific competitors, laid out consistently
across the same set of dimensions. Its job is to answer a concrete
question — how does this stack up against the alternatives a buyer is
actually weighing — not to describe the industry in general or to
assess the company in isolation.

## Key components

- **Comparison dimensions** — the specific criteria that actually
  drive a buyer's decision (price, core features, integrations,
  support, ease of setup, security posture), chosen for relevance to
  the buyer rather than to whichever criteria happen to favor the
  company's own product.
- **Named competitors** — specific, identified alternatives, not a
  vague "the market" — including direct competitors and, deliberately,
  indirect ones (see below).
- **Consistent comparison across the same dimensions** — every
  competitor evaluated against the same criteria in the same format,
  so the comparison is actually apples-to-apples rather than each
  competitor described in whatever terms happen to be available.
- **Sourced, dated evidence** — each claim traceable to something
  concrete (a pricing page, a docs page, a review site, a sales call
  note) and dated, since competitor offerings change and a comparison
  with no date attached can't be trusted as current.

## Common pitfalls

- **Dimensions chosen to flatter the company's own product** — picking
  criteria the company happens to win on, rather than the criteria
  buyers actually weigh, produces a comparison that looks favorable
  internally and falls apart the moment a prospect checks it against
  their own priorities.
- **Stale information gathered once and never updated** — competitor
  pricing, features, and positioning change; a comparison built at one
  point in time and reused for months quietly drifts from reality and
  can actively mislead a sales team relying on it.
- **Ignoring indirect competitors and substitutes** — a spreadsheet
  someone builds themselves, a manual process, or a different category
  of tool that solves the same underlying problem a different way
  competes for the same buyer decision even if it's never named as a
  "competitor" in the traditional sense; leaving these out understates
  the real alternative set.
- **Treated as a one-time deliverable instead of a maintained
  reference** — built for a single pitch or launch and then never
  touched again, even as the competitive landscape moves.
- **Comparison used only to reassure the team, not to inform the
  product or pitch** — a comparison that concludes "we win everywhere"
  every time is more likely miscalibrated than actually accurate, and
  stops being useful for making real decisions.
