# Rainy Day Folder

A rainy day folder is a place where one collects kind messages, praise,
photos, and good memories as they arrive, so that on a bad day they are
already gathered. It is an idea widely shared online, and nobody needs
a particular source to use it.

It is a small self-care tool. It is not a treatment.

## Input required

- **Where things already live:** email, chat, photo library, notes,
  paper cards.
- **Preferred format:** a digital folder, an album, or a physical box.
- **Kinds of content** that lift the user, and kinds to exclude.
- **People** whose messages matter.
- **Privacy needs:** who might see it.

## Questions to ask first

Ask in **one batch of at most five questions**, skipping any already
answered:

1. Do you prefer a digital folder, a photo album, a paper box, or all
   three?
2. Where do kind messages, praise, and good photos currently collect?
3. What kind of thing helps on a bad day: words, pictures, memories,
   humour, or something else?
4. Is there anything that should stay out, such as a message that hurts
   in hindsight?
5. Who, if anyone, would you want to know about it?

## Method

1. **Pick one home.** A single folder or album with a clear name. Digital
   is quick to feed; a physical box is good for cards and notes. Keep
   the structure flat.
2. **Seed it today.** Search past messages and photos for the best ten.
   Praise from colleagues, thanks from friends, thank-you cards, a
   photo from a good day, a funny voice note.
3. **Add a capture habit.** When a kind message arrives, screenshot or
   save it straight away. Pair this with a recurring prompt, such as the
   end of the week or [[friday-highlights]], to add one or two items.
4. **Add context.** Under each item, a line on why it matters or what
   happened that day. On a bad day, context does the remembering.
5. **Include the user's own words** where they want to: a note to a
   future self about what is true when things feel dark.
6. **Make it open-able.** Put a shortcut on the phone home screen, with a
   plain name the user will recognize at a low point. Keep the bad-day
   path to two taps.
7. **Set the opening rule.** Open it on a bad day, before a hard
   conversation, or on a hard anniversary. Take five or ten minutes, then
   do one small next step: water, air, or a call to someone.
8. **Curate gently.** Remove items that start to sting; do not delete
   original messages, just keep them out of the folder.
9. **Back it up** with the user's usual backup and keep it private.

## Output format

- **Setup plan:** location, name, and structure.
- **Starter list:** where to search for the first ten items.
- **Capture rule** with its trigger.
- **Opening protocol** with the next-step prompt.
- **Privacy and backup note.**

## Ground rules

- **Not treatment.** A folder can comfort. It does not treat depression,
  anxiety, or grief.
- **Persistent low mood** (lasting weeks, or affecting sleep, eating, or
  daily life) is a reason to see a doctor or mental health professional.
- **Thoughts of self-harm or suicide:** contact local emergency services
  or a crisis line now, or reach someone trusted. Ask the user's country
  and point to the official service; do not guess a number.
- **Do not frame the idea** as belonging to any person or organization.
- **Respect other people's privacy** when saving their messages, and do
  not share the folder without asking.
- **Never write fake praise** for the user.

## Self-check before answering

- Does the plan give both a seed step and a capture habit?
- Can the folder be opened in two taps on a bad day?
- Is there a next step after opening, not just wallowing?
- Did I add the mental health safety note?
- Did I avoid crediting the idea to a person?

## Common pitfalls

- **Building it and never adding** to it.
- **Burying it** in nested folders.
- **Including painful memories** with the good ones.
- **Using it to avoid** reaching out to a real person.
- **Treating it as therapy** for a long-running problem.
- **Exposing private messages** on a shared device.

## Example prompt

> Help me set up a rainy day folder on my phone and laptop. Tell me
> where to find the first ten items, how to add new ones as they come
> in, and what I should do after I open it.
