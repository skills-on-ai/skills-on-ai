# Training Needs Analysis

A training needs analysis identifies the actual gap between what
people currently know or can do and what they need to know or do,
before any training is designed. Its job is to confirm there's a real,
specific gap — and that training is actually the right way to close
it — rather than starting from an assumption that training is needed
and working backward to justify it.

## Key components

- **The specific performance gap** — a concrete description of what
  people currently do versus what they need to do, not a vague sense
  that "people need more training."
- **Root cause of the gap** — why the gap exists. See
  [[root-cause-analysis]]: not every performance gap is a training
  problem. The same symptom can come from a broken process, a missing
  or unusable tool, unclear expectations, or a lack of incentive to
  perform the behavior correctly — none of which a training session
  fixes.
- **Target audience and current skill level** — who actually needs the
  training and what they already know, so the program is designed for
  their real starting point rather than an assumed one.
- **Success measure** — how it will be confirmed, after training is
  delivered, that the gap actually closed — not just that the training
  happened.

## Why confirming training is the right intervention matters

Training is often the default response to any reported performance
problem, because it's visible, easy to commission, and feels like
action. But if the actual cause is a broken process, a tool that
doesn't support the desired behavior, or an incentive structure that
rewards the wrong thing, training the people involved doesn't fix
any of that — it spends real time and budget while the underlying
cause keeps producing the same gap. Confirming the root cause before
designing training is what separates an intervention that actually
closes the gap from one that just adds a session to people's calendars.

## Common pitfalls

- **Training as the default response** — commissioning or delivering
  training automatically whenever a performance problem is reported,
  without first checking whether the gap is actually a training
  problem at all.
- **Needs assessed from assumption, not from the people involved** —
  building the analysis on what leadership assumes the gap is, rather
  than gathering input from the people who will actually take the
  training and the managers who observe the gap day to day.
- **No way to measure the outcome afterward** — running the training
  and moving on, with no baseline or follow-up measure to confirm
  whether the specific gap identified at the start actually closed.
- **Gap described too vaguely to design against** — "needs better
  communication skills" doesn't tell a curriculum designer what
  specific behavior to target or how to know it improved.
