# Sports Nutrition Fitness

Sports nutrition is the practice of fueling and supporting the body's
training and recovery through diet — a discipline that supports every
other fitness goal, since inadequate nutrition can undermine even a
well-designed training program.

## Protein and recovery

Adequate protein intake supports muscle repair and growth following
training — active individuals, especially those doing regular
resistance training, generally need more protein than a sedentary
person. Spreading protein intake across the day, rather than
concentrating it in one meal, is generally considered more effective
for supporting ongoing muscle repair.

## Pre- and post-workout fueling

- **Pre-workout** — a meal with carbohydrates (for readily available
  energy) eaten with enough time to digest before training reduces the
  chance of low energy or gastrointestinal discomfort during a
  session.
- **Post-workout** — combining protein and carbohydrates after
  training supports muscle repair and replenishes energy stores used
  during the session; the exact timing window matters less than most
  popular advice once suggested, but eating a reasonable meal within a
  few hours of training is a solid general practice.

## Hydration

Even mild dehydration can measurably reduce performance and increase
perceived effort during training. Hydration needs scale with training
intensity, duration, and environmental heat — a fixed daily water
target is a reasonable baseline, but demanding or long training
sessions typically require more.

## Common pitfalls

- **Under-fueling relative to training demands** — chronic
  under-eating relative to training load undermines recovery,
  performance, and (for many goals) muscle growth.
- **Ignoring hydration until thirst appears** — thirst is a lagging
  indicator of dehydration; proactive hydration, especially around
  demanding sessions, performs better than reacting to thirst alone.
- **Overemphasizing narrow timing windows over overall daily intake**
  — total daily protein and calorie intake generally matters more to
  results than precisely timing a single post-workout meal.
- **Relying heavily on supplements over whole-food nutrition** —
  supplements can fill specific gaps, but they're not a substitute for
  an adequate overall diet.
