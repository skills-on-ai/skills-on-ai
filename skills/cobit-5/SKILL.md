# COBIT 5

COBIT 5 is a comprehensive IT governance and management framework from ISACA. It suits enterprises that focus heavily on risk management, regulatory compliance, cybersecurity, and overall corporate governance. Note that ISACA later published COBIT 2019, which updates it; confirm which version the user's organization or auditor requires.

## Input required

- The enterprise, its stakeholders, and its governance structure.
- The COBIT version required (COBIT 5 or COBIT 2019) and the official material available.
- Key drivers: regulation, risk appetite, audit findings, cybersecurity concerns.
- Current IT processes and controls.

## Questions to ask first

1. Which COBIT version is required or in use?
2. Which stakeholder needs and enterprise goals drive the work?
3. Which regulations or audits apply?
4. Which IT processes are in scope?
5. Who owns governance decisions, and who owns management?

## Method

1. **Separate governance from management.** Governance sets direction and checks results; management plans, builds, runs, and monitors. Assign each to the right body.
2. **Start from stakeholder needs.** Translate them into enterprise goals, then IT-related goals, then the processes that serve them, using the official goals cascade.
3. **Select processes.** Pick the processes in scope from the official process model, and define owners.
4. **Assess capability.** Assess current process capability and set targets, using the method in the official material.
5. **Address risk and compliance.** Tie processes to the risk register and to legal and regulatory duties.
6. **Plan improvements.** Prioritize gaps by risk and value, and assign actions.
7. **Monitor.** Define measures and reporting to the board or governing body.

## Output format

- Goals cascade table: stakeholder need, enterprise goal, IT goal, process.
- Process scope and ownership table.
- Capability gap list with priorities.
- Roadmap and reporting plan.

## Ground rules

- Do not quote process lists, capability levels, or principles beyond what the user supplied or official ISACA text confirms; they differ between COBIT 5 and COBIT 2019.
- Do not invent compliance claims.
- COBIT is a framework, not a certifiable standard for organizations.

## Self-check before answering

- Is the COBIT version stated?
- Are governance and management responsibilities separated?
- Is each process traced to a stakeholder need?

## Common pitfalls

- **Adopting every process** instead of those that matter.
- **Mixing COBIT 5 and COBIT 2019 terms.**
- **Treating it as a checklist** rather than tailoring to enterprise goals.
- **No board-level ownership.**

## Example prompt

"Use COBIT to map our regulatory and cybersecurity goals to IT processes, and propose a capability improvement roadmap."
