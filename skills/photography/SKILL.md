# Photography Hobby

Photography is the practice of capturing images using a camera —
approachable with just a smartphone, and open-ended in how deep the
technical and creative craft can go from there.

## The exposure basics

Every camera, from a phone to a professional body, controls the same
three things to determine exposure:

- **Aperture** — how wide the lens opening is; wider (a smaller
  f-number) lets in more light and blurs the background more.
- **Shutter speed** — how long the sensor is exposed; faster freezes
  motion, slower can blur it (intentionally, for effects like flowing
  water) or introduce camera shake.
- **ISO** — the sensor's sensitivity to light; higher ISO brightens a
  dark scene but adds visual noise.

Learning to balance these three deliberately, rather than always
leaving the camera on full auto, is what turns "point and shoot" into
photography as a craft.

## Composition matters more than gear

A well-composed photo from a phone usually beats a poorly composed one
from an expensive camera. A few durable principles: the rule of
thirds (placing the subject off-center), leading lines that draw the
eye into the frame, and paying attention to what's in the background,
not just the subject.

## Common pitfalls

- **Buying expensive gear before learning composition and light** —
  the camera matters far less than understanding how to see and frame
  a shot; an entry-level camera in the hands of someone who understands
  light will consistently outperform an expensive one in the hands of
  someone who doesn't.
- **Shooting only in harsh midday light** — the hour after sunrise and
  before sunset ("golden hour") produces dramatically more flattering,
  directional light than the flat, harsh light of midday sun.
- **Not backing up photos** — losing a memory card or a phone with
  years of photos and no backup is a common, avoidable loss.
- **Over-editing** — heavy-handed filters and edits can make a photo
  look artificial; small, deliberate adjustments usually read better
  than dramatic ones.
