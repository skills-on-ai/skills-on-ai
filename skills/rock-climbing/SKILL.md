# Rock Climbing Hobby

Rock climbing is the hobby of ascending rock faces or artificial walls
using hands, feet, and (beyond simple bouldering) ropes and safety
equipment — most beginners start at an indoor climbing gym before
progressing to outdoor climbing.

## Starting indoors

Indoor climbing gyms offer a controlled, safety-managed environment to
learn technique and, for roped climbing, belaying (managing the rope
for a climbing partner's safety) with instruction. Bouldering (short,
unroped climbs over a padded floor) is a common lower-barrier entry
point, since it doesn't require learning rope systems to get started.

## Technique over strength

New climbers often over-rely on arm strength, which tires quickly;
efficient climbing relies heavily on footwork, body positioning, and
weight distribution over the legs. Learning to trust and precisely
place feet, and to keep arms relatively straight rather than
constantly pulling, is one of the biggest technique shifts for
beginners.

## Safety systems

Roped climbing depends on correctly tied knots, a properly fitted
harness, and a competent, attentive belayer — these fundamentals are
non-negotiable and are why gyms require a belay certification/check
before letting new climbers belay unsupervised. Moving to outdoor
climbing adds further considerations (route-finding, anchor building,
rock quality assessment) that go well beyond gym basics and are worth
learning from an experienced mentor or guide rather than purely
self-taught.

## Common pitfalls

- **Over-relying on arm strength instead of footwork** — this is one of
  the most common and limiting beginner habits; deliberately focusing
  on foot placement improves efficiency and endurance faster than
  building grip strength alone.
- **Skipping proper safety checks before roped climbing** — checking
  knots, harness fit, and belay device setup every time (not just when
  new to it) is standard practice, not excessive caution.
- **Moving to outdoor climbing before building a solid safety and
  technique foundation indoors** — outdoor climbing introduces real
  risks (rock quality, anchor building, weather) that gym climbing
  doesn't prepare for on its own.
- **Progressing grade difficulty too quickly** — climbing routes well
  above current skill level increases both injury risk and the
  likelihood of discouraging falls.
