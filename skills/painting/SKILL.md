# Painting Hobby

Painting is the practice of applying pigment to a surface to create an
image — a hobby that spans a wide range of mediums, each with a
distinct feel, learning curve, and set of materials.

## Choosing a medium

- **Watercolor** — inexpensive to start, fast-drying, forgiving of
  small mistakes but unforgiving of large ones (hard to fully "undo" a
  layer); good for beginners who want low upfront cost.
- **Acrylic** — fast-drying, water-cleanup, very forgiving (mistakes
  can be painted over once dry); a common recommended starting medium.
- **Oil** — slow-drying (which allows blending over a longer working
  time), richer color, but requires solvents for cleanup and a longer
  learning curve for handling.

## Fundamentals that transfer across mediums

- **Value before color** — getting the light-and-dark relationships
  right (value) matters more to a painting reading correctly than
  getting the exact hue right; many beginner paintings fail on value,
  not color choice.
- **Working from reference** — painting from a photo or still life,
  rather than purely from imagination, builds observational skill
  faster early on.
- **Layering and patience** — most paintings improve through multiple
  passes, not a single attempt at getting everything right at once.

## Common pitfalls

- **Buying a large set of expensive materials before knowing what
  medium suits them** — a small set of basic colors and one medium is
  enough to learn the fundamentals that transfer everywhere else.
- **Focusing on color before getting values right** — see above; this
  is one of the most common reasons a painting doesn't "read" correctly
  even when the colors are reasonable.
- **Never painting from reference** — relying purely on imagination
  before building observational skill from real subjects or photos
  slows learning considerably.
- **Being discouraged by early results** — like most skills, early
  paintings are rarely satisfying; consistent practice, not talent
  alone, is what drives visible improvement.
